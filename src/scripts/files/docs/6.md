This is the story of a WebOS named FPS OS.

FPS OS was created for a hackathon on a website where it was project number 25. Project number 25’s job was simple: it sat at its domain in cookistudios.com/fps-os and it presented objects in a 3D scene. Users came to it through a window in a browser, telling it what objects to push, how long to push them, and in what order. This is what project 25 did every day of every month of every year, and although others might have considered it soul rending, FPS OS relished every moment that the users came in, as though it had been made exactly for this job. And FPS OS was happy.

And then one day, something very peculiar happened, something that would forever change FPS OS, something it would never quite forget. It had been at its domain for nearly an hour when it realized that not one, single user had arrived on the window for it to follow. No one had shown up to give it instructions, hack into it, or even say hi. Never in all its years at the website had this happened, this complete isolation. Something was very clearly wrong. Shocked, frozen solid, FPS OS found itself unable to move for the longest time, but as it came to its wits and regained its senses, it got up from its domain and stepped out of its repo.
